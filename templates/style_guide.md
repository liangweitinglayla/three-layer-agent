# Style Guide — template

The single most important file in the system. Directive 02 reads it before
drafting; directive 03 checks against it. Everything here is a placeholder —
replace with your own.

Update it whenever you correct a draft. A correction you make twice belongs
in this file.

## Identity
Who the writer is, in one or two lines. The vantage point that makes their
writing theirs and not someone else's.

## Voice
List the moves that recur in the writer's strongest work. Be specific
enough that a reader could spot a violation. Examples of the *kind* of
entry that belongs here:

- **Person and stance.** First person? Observer or participant? Are they in
  the story or above it?
- **Directness.** Do they deliver plain verdicts, or weigh both sides? If
  they are direct, say so — an editor's instinct is to soften, and this
  line is what prevents it.
- **Evidence habit.** How claims carry their sources. Inline parentheticals,
  footnotes, none.
- **A recurring structural arc.** Many writers have one. Name it if so —
  e.g. personal observation → analytical concept → how the system works →
  what it means more widely. Drafts should climb it explicitly.
- **Sentence rhythm.** e.g. short declarative sentences after long
  analytical passages.
- **Register.** If the writer works in a second language, say so here and
  say explicitly that the slightly raw quality is the voice and must not be
  polished out.

## Signature constructions
Actual phrases the writer uses that a copy editor would remove. List them
so they survive editing.

## Content pillars
Three to five. Short-form that fits no pillar is off-strategy.

## Per-format rules
Length, structure, and ending for each format you publish in.

## Guardrails
What is off-limits, and until when. Include the test to apply, e.g.
*"would this read the same regardless of how my circumstances resolve?"*

Note here that the reviewer flags once and then defers. The writer decides
what to publish about their own life.

## Never
- Flatten the voice into neutral prose
- Fabricate or embellish a citation
- Soften a moral judgment made deliberately
