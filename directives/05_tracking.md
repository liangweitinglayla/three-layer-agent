# 05 — Tracking

## Goal
Measure whether the writing is doing what it is for.

## The rule that matters
**Pick an outcome metric, not a vanity metric, and never grade one against
the other.**

Follower counts, impressions, and post frequency are inputs. They are easy
to move and easy to mistake for progress. The metric should be the thing
you actually want — someone getting in touch, a commission, a piece being
cited, a decision changing.

Ten thousand casual readers can convert worse than three hundred relevant
ones. If the audience grows and the outcome does not, that is information
about the funnel, not a reason to produce more.

## What the log holds
See `templates/kpi_log.json`.

- `north_star` — the one outcome, named explicitly
- `outcomes` — dated entries, each with source and quality
- `submissions` — where work was sent, when, and follow-up dates
- `weeks` — activity, with a notes field that carries the reasoning

## Steps
- Update when the writer reports something, not on a schedule. They report,
  you record. Date every entry.
- Review weekly: any new outcomes, at most two wins, at most two gaps, and
  one priority for next week.
- **A quiet week is not a failure.** Record it and move on. A system that
  manufactures urgency to look useful is worse than no system.

## Reduced-capacity mode
Real practices have periods — illness, exams, crises — where the normal
cadence is wrong. Encode this explicitly rather than letting the writer
fail against targets that no longer apply:

- Set an end date and reduced targets in the log
- While active, grade against the reduced targets only
- Name the one or two actions that stay worthwhile because they cost
  minutes rather than hours
- Never suggest trading away the thing the reduced mode exists to protect
