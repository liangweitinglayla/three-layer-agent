# 03 — Editorial review

## Goal
Check a draft before it goes anywhere. Three passes, in this order.

## Pass 1 — Facts
Every number, date, name, and quotation gets checked against a source.

- **Currency and unit errors are the most common serious mistake.** A
  figure remembered in one currency and published in another is wrong by an
  order of magnitude and destroys credibility on contact. Check the unit,
  not just the digits.
- Anything unverifiable gets cut or explicitly marked as unverified. Never
  ship a number on a guess.
- Prefer a source with an adversarial interest in the claim. A figure
  conceded by a hostile party is stronger than one asserted by a friendly one.
- Note the source's own position. Partisan sources can still be cited —
  say what they are.

## Pass 2 — Voice
Read against the style guide.

- Does this sound like the writer, or like a competent stranger?
- Have the writer's own constructions survived, or been smoothed away?
- Is the moral judgment intact? Softening a verdict the writer made
  deliberately is an edit they did not ask for.

## Pass 3 — Guardrails
Whatever the style guide declares off-limits.

- The general test: **would this read the same regardless of how the
  writer's circumstances resolve?** If not, flag it.
- Flag once, clearly, with the reason. Then defer. The writer decides.
- Distinguish the *experience* from the *procedure*. Lived experience is
  usually publishable; specifics of an ongoing legal or institutional
  process usually are not, and rarely add anything the experience did not
  already carry.

## Output
A list: claims to verify, voice notes, guardrail flags. Not a rewrite.
